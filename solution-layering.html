The concepts, connected
Managed vs Unmanaged — and why only one is reversible

Unmanaged = editable source. Your dev environment is unmanaged; direct edits land in the Active layer, which sits on top of everything.

Managed = sealed output. You export managed and import into test/prod. Its components are locked and, crucially, removable — uninstall the solution and its layer lifts off cleanly, revealing whatever was beneath.

  • An unmanaged layer can never be "uninstalled" — there's nothing to remove it from. You can only delete the component or remove the active customization (which drops you to the managed value below).
  • This is why shipping everything unmanaged to production is an architect's red flag: it can't be cleanly upgraded or rolled back.
"My managed update won't show up"

Classic production mystery. You ship a fix in a managed solution, it imports fine, but the old behaviour persists.

Cause: someone once edited that component directly in production, creating an Unmanaged (Active) layer on top. The active layer wins over every managed layer below — including your new one. Your update is installed; it's just buried.

Fix: on the component, Remove Active Customization. The active layer lifts off and your managed value surfaces. Then enforce "no direct edits in prod" so it doesn't recur. Try the first scenario button above to see it.

Two ISV solutions touch the same field

When two managed solutions both set the same property, the one imported later lands in a higher layer and wins — order of installation becomes behaviour. This is why publisher prefixes (avpx_, av_) and clear solution boundaries matter: they keep ownership legible and collisions rare.

Architect move: don't let your solution redefine components another solution owns unless you mean to. Depend on them, extend alongside them, but avoid silent overrides you'll forget about in a year.

Where Dynamics 365's own apps sit

Dynamics 365 Sales, Customer Service, Field Service — these are just managed solutions sitting near the bottom of the stack, on top of the base platform. Your solutions layer above them.

That's the whole trick: there is no separate "Dynamics engine." Microsoft's apps and your PREXA365 customisations are the same kind of thing, resolved by the same layering rules. Once you see that, the platform stops being magic.
